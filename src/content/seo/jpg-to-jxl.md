Searching for **jpg to jxl** usually means one thing: shrink a JPEG library without mailing every frame to a third-party converter. PictoJXL decodes the JPG on your device, builds a new JPEG XL bitstream from your Quality and Effort, and shows the tradeoff in a split preview.

## What you gain over 1990s JPEG

Classic JPEG is still the lingua franca of photos. JPEG XL targets the same jobs with denser bitstreams and cleaner behavior at aggressive sizes. Skin, skies, and soft gradients are where you should stare while you tune—blocking and banding show up there first.

Catalog pages, newsletter heroes, and local archives all benefit once you confirm the look. The size badge next to the encode tells you whether the cut is worth shipping.

## What happens after you drop a JPG

1. The studio decodes the file in-browser.
2. Workers encode JPEG XL using your panel settings.
3. The preview updates so you can scrub detail with the slider.
4. You download `.jxl` or push a batch through the [bulk converter](/bulk-jxl-converter).

There is no upload queue and no temporary cloud copy. Heavy personal libraries stay on the laptop that already holds them.

## Practical workflow

1. Add `.jpg` / `.jpeg` files above.
2. Set Quality and Effort—or Lossless if you want a bit-exact wrap of the decoded raster.
3. Check faces, text overlays, and gradients at 100% and 200% zoom.
4. Export, then reuse the same settings in Queue for the rest of the shoot.
